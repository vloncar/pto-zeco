GATED DELTANET — bottom half.  Target 1:30, hard floor 1:00.

[0:00 · 15s] WHAT IT IS
Gated DeltaNet is the attention layer inside Qwen3.5 and 3.6 — it stands in for
most of the ordinary attention in those models, so how fast it runs is how fast
they run. There is already a hand-tuned version of it in production, written
directly against the chip.

[0:15 · 20s] WHAT WE DID
We rebuilt those six kernels in the compiler's own language. Not a new algorithm
— deliberately the same one, the same six stages, the same accuracy target. That
is what makes the comparison mean something: a difference is the compiler, not a
different idea.

[0:35 · 25s] THE RESULT  ← the milestone
Every stage agrees with the reference to the precision the hardware allows, and
end to end the generated code is slightly ahead — 1,463 microseconds against
1,572 for the hand-written original. The chart is their own benchmark, run
unchanged. Two stages are clearly ahead, two are level, two are a little behind.

[1:00 · 20s] THE PART WORTH REPEATING
Rewriting the triangular solve made us re-derive what it was doing, and that
turned up two shortcuts the original author had not taken. Both are improvements
to THEIR code, not ours, and they are going back to that team. That is the
argument for working at this level: it makes the algorithm visible again.

[1:20 · 10s] NEXT
Fuse the six stages into one kernel, then variable-length batches.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF ASKED "is it production ready?": not yet — one sequence at a time, fixed head
count. The next two pieces of work are exactly the features that close that gap.

IF ASKED "why is the last stage behind?": it makes more trips between the matrix
unit and the vector unit than the hand-written version, which routes those
through a different memory. That is a known request into the compiler, not a
fault in the kernel.
